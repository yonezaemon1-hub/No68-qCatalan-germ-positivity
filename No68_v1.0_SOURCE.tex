\begin{abstract}
Drew Armstrong introduced $q$-Catalan germs and reported verifying their coefficientwise nonnegativity through $a=20$ \cite{Armstrong2026}. We report exact finite computations for every germ with $3\le a\le32$, comprising $6{,}426$ admissible germs, including $4{,}918$ for $21\le a\le32$. No negative coefficient occurred in the checked range. The extension uses bounded dynamic programming, a separately implemented inclusion--exclusion method, exact division by $[a]_q$, arbitrary-precision Python cross-checks, and frozen reproducibility records. The computations were reproduced in a reported independent AI-assisted Linux audit and compared with author-operated Windows execution records; the reported fresh Windows run contains environment and exit-code metadata, but its original log file is not archived here. The provenance and scope of these reports are distinguished below. The established claim concerns only a finite range of $a$, not the unrestricted conjecture. The equivalence and implications proved in Armstrong's paper give bounded instances of standard partitions and Johnson statistics, without a new general proof. Historical priority beyond the cited source is unresolved; this manuscript is not peer reviewed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and source conventions}
We use the numbering of the open-access \emph{Annals of Combinatorics} article by Armstrong \cite{Armstrong2026}, published 28 April 2026: Definition~8.2, Proposition~8.3, Conjectures~8.4 and~9.2. The arXiv submission history identifies version~4 as revised 17 April 2026 and version~3 as revised 29 September 2025 \cite{ArmstrongArxivV4}; the v4 PDF title page itself still says ``September 2025''. These are different date fields, not conflicting manuscript versions. The v4 PDF and published article use the cited Section~8 numbering, whereas a v3 HTML rendering uses a different numbering system. We therefore fix the published article numbering throughout.

For an integer $a\ge3$, set
\[
M=(a-1)^2,\quad [a]_q=1+q+\cdots+q^{a-1},\quad
\mathbf{x}=(x_1,\ldots,x_{a-1})\in\{0,\ldots,a-1\}^{a-1}.
\]
Write $S(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}x_i$ and $T(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{a-1}ix_i$. For $1\le c\le M$ with $\gcd(a,c)=1$, define
\[
p_a(c)=\max\{d<c:\gcd(a,d)=1\},\qquad
G_{a,c}(q)=\frac{1}{[a]_q}
\sum_{\substack{0\le x_i\le a-1\\p_a(c)<S(\mathbf{x})\le c}}q^{T(\mathbf{x})}.
\tag{1}\label{eq:germ}
\]
In particular $p_a(1)=-1$. This is the germ of Definition~8.2 \cite{Armstrong2026}; Armstrong's Theorem~8.1 guarantees $G_{a,c}(q)\in\ZZ[q]$.

\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted, finite parameter range]\label{thm:finite}
For every integer $3\le a\le32$ and every $1\le c\le(a-1)^2$ with $\gcd(a,c)=1$, exact computation of the polynomial in \eqref{eq:germ} yields
\[
G_{a,c}(q)\in\NN[q].
\]
The complete checked family comprises $6{,}426$ germs. For $21\le a\le32$ there are $4{,}918$ germs, all with zero negative coefficients; the subrange $28\le a\le32$ contains $2{,}647$ germs.
\end{theorem}
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite} is a reproducible, machine-verified \emph{finite computation statement}, rather than a pen-and-paper proof of Conjecture~8.4 for arbitrary $a$. The interval $21\le a\le32$ extends the computational range explicitly documented in Armstrong's published article, which states that the germ conjecture was tested through $a=20$ \cite{Armstrong2026}; this does not establish exclusive historical priority.

\begin{lemma}[count of admissible germs]\label{lem:count}
For $a\ge3$, the number of admissible indices $c$ in \eqref{eq:germ} is $(a-2)\varphi(a)+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Since $(a-1)^2=a(a-2)+1$, the indices $1,\ldots,a(a-2)$ consist of $a-2$ complete residue blocks modulo $a$, each containing $\varphi(a)$ integers coprime to $a$. The final index $a(a-2)+1$ is congruent to $1\pmod a$ and contributes one more.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact computational method}
For each $a$, let $D_i(s,w)$ count vectors $(x_1,\ldots,x_i)$ with $0\le x_j\le a-1$, $\sum_{j=1}^i x_j=s$, and $\sum_{j=1}^i jx_j=w$. Starting from $D_0(0,0)=1$, one program uses the bounded recurrence
\[
D_i(s,w)=D_{i-1}(s,w)+D_i(s-1,w-i)-D_{i-1}(s-a,w-ai),\tag{2}
\]
with negative subscripts interpreted as zero. A separate C++ implementation first counts unrestricted vectors and imposes $x_i<a$ by inclusion--exclusion; this is an algorithmically distinct numerator calculation. The Python verifier and C++ recurrence use the same bounded recurrence and are independently implemented, not independent numerator algorithms.

For each admissible $c$ set\[
P_{a,c}(w)=\sum_{s=p_a(c)+1}^{c}D_{a-1}(s,w),\qquad
G_{a,c}(q)=\sum_{w\ge0}Q_{a,c}(w)q^w.
\]
Exact division by $[a]_q$ uses
\[
Q_{a,c}(w)=P_{a,c}(w)-P_{a,c}(w-1)+Q_{a,c}(w-a),\tag{3}
\]
with out-of-range entries zero. This is the coefficient identity obtained from $(1-q^a)G_{a,c}(q)=(1-q)\sum_wP_{a,c}(w)q^w$. Every computed quotient coefficient is tested for sign. All programs use this same algebraic quotient identity; the C++ verifier additionally tests exact numerator reconstruction from the quotient and checks the vanishing quotient tail. Thus equation (3) is checked by reconstruction, but the separate numerator methods do not constitute independent derivations of the division identity.

The frozen C++ checker also verifies the evaluation identity $\sum_c G_{a,c}(1)=a^{a-2}$, and the reciprocal-pair symmetry of the germs. Signed 256-bit arithmetic is used in its validated range $a\le32$; the archive records a conservative upper bound under 191 bits for each unrestricted intermediate DP count and under 165 bits for the quotient recurrence. A separately implemented standard-library Python checker uses arbitrary-precision integers. Small cases are independently compared to Armstrong's Table~1, while the separately reported AI-assisted audit additionally enumerated the definitions directly for $a=6,7,8$.

\section{Consequences from Armstrong's theorems}
Armstrong's published article proves that Conjecture~8.4 (germ positivity) and Conjecture~9.2 (standard partitions) are equivalent. It also proves that either yields Conjecture~7.2 (Johnson statistics) and Conjecture~1.1 (coefficientwise monotonicity of rational $q$-Catalan polynomials), through the implications shown in its Section~10 \cite{Armstrong2026}.
\begin{corollary}[bounded instances of Armstrong's conjectures]\label{cor:consequences}
For each fixed integer $3\le a\le32$, the finite verification of Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}, together with Armstrong's established equivalences and implications, yields the corresponding fixed-$a$ instances of Conjectures~9.2 and~7.2. In particular, for all positive $b<b'$ with $\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(a,b')=1$,
\[
\Cat(a,b')_q-\Cat(a,b)_q\in\NN[q].
\]
\end{corollary}
\noindent These consequences are applications of \cite{Armstrong2026}, not independent proofs of the conjectures for arbitrary $a$. The stronger ribbon-partition conjecture (Conjecture~10.2) does \emph{not} follow from germ positivity and is not claimed here.

\section{Scope, novelty and audit status}
The claimed extension is precisely a finite computation for Armstrong's \emph{germs}, not the previously known nonnegativity of rational $q$-Catalan polynomials themselves. A limited public literature search through 14 November 2026 identified no direct published duplicate of the range $a=21,\ldots,32$, but was not exhaustive and cannot exclude unpublished or differently indexed computations. Hence \textbf{NOVELTY: INCONCLUSIVE}; \textbf{REFERENCES: PARTIAL}. The independent audits reported here were AI-assisted; no human peer review was performed. The unrestricted statement for arbitrary $a$ is unresolved by this work. Nothing herein asserts a proven Fibonacci connection.
